Keep pressing ↑ at 100% to feel the rubber band.

Why springs, not curves

Click Toggle repeatedly while the dots are still moving. Today's NumberAnimation restarts at zero speed on every interruption, so you get a kink in the graph. The spring keeps its velocity and turns around smoothly. That's most of the "Apple feel".

What the Dynamic Island actually is

Three presentations

  • Compact: leading and trailing content on either side of the pill (art + waveform).
  • Minimal: with two live activities, one stays attached and the other detaches into a small circle.
  • Expanded: long-press opens rich controls. A tap opens the app.

How the motion works

  • Width, height and corner radius are separate springs on one black shape.
  • Content cross-fades with a blur, slightly scaled. It never just swaps.
  • Splitting and merging look like a liquid "metaball" (here: an SVG blur + alpha threshold).

Why it works

One fixed place for live state, so you always know where to look. The island holds long-running state (media, recording). Notifications stack on the right, and volume gets its own pill at the bottom, so nothing fights for the center.

What maps onto Rashell

  • MediaState → compact + expanded player
  • ScreenshotState / RecordToolbar → recording timer bubble
  • VolumeOsd → bottom-center pill, separate from the island
  • NotificationPopup → top-right cards with Reply / Open, swipe to dismiss
  • MediaDownloadState → progress ring; Translate → result peek
  • DND on, a pomodoro or timer → minimal bubble

How it would land in QML

Qt's SpringAnimation uses different units, and NumberAnimation can't preserve velocity. A ~30-line Spring.qml driven by FrameAnimation reproduces exactly what this page runs. The values below follow the tuner.
